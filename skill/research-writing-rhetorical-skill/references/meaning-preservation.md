# Meaning-Preservation Audit

Meaning preservation is the highest-priority requirement. A more elegant sentence is a failure if it changes the research claim.

## Preserve these dimensions

### Substantive proposition

The central claim, comparison, or question must remain the same.

### Direction

Do not reverse increases, decreases, advantages, disadvantages, or group differences.

### Magnitude

Do not turn a small difference into a substantial one or remove quantitative qualifiers.

### Uncertainty

Preserve hedges, confidence intervals, imprecision, ambiguity, and conditional language when they are analytically necessary.

### Statistical status

Preserve significance, nonsignificance, robustness, model dependence, and distinction between point and interval estimates.

### Causal status

Do not strengthen association into mechanism or causation. Do not weaken identified causal effects without reason.

### Comparison structure

Preserve the reference group, baseline, treatment, comparator, direction of comparison, and evaluation criterion.

### Population and setting

Do not broaden the sample, target population, institution, country, or deployment setting.

### Temporal structure

Preserve whether the evidence is cross-sectional, longitudinal, prospective, retrospective, or dynamic.

### Scope and boundary conditions

Retain conditions under which the claim applies or fails.

### Author position

Preserve whether the author is asserting, proposing, questioning, criticizing, or cautiously interpreting.

## Critical failures

Any of the following requires a new revision:

- invented result;
- invented citation;
- invented method or dataset;
- invented mechanism;
- fabricated novelty or contribution;
- changed numerical value;
- reversed direction;
- changed statistical significance;
- strengthened causality;
- broadened generalization;
- removed limitation or uncertainty;
- changed comparison group;
- changed policy recommendation;
- resolved an ambiguity that the source text leaves unresolved.

## Compression risks

When making prose concise, do not delete:

- `may`, `could`, `appears`, or `is consistent with` when they reflect evidence limits;
- sample or subgroup labels;
- time windows;
- baseline definitions;
- conditions under which results hold;
- distinctions among statistical, practical, and policy significance;
- necessary exceptions.

Example:

Before: `The findings may partly reflect differences in sample composition.`

Unsafe compression: `Sample composition explains the findings.`

Safe revision: `Differences in sample composition may partly account for the pattern.`

## Specificity risks

Do not add specificity merely because it sounds more professional.

Before: `The method performed better in some settings.`

Unsafe revision: `The method reduced false actions in rare-outcome settings.`

The revision invents a setting and outcome unless the surrounding context supplies them.

Safe revision: `The method performed better in the evaluated settings.`

## Literature-claim risks

Do not change:

`The reviewed studies provide limited evidence...`

into:

`The literature provides no evidence...`

The second claim is broader and stronger.

## Result-to-interpretation risks

Separate observation from explanation where necessary.

Observation: `Only 22.57% of threshold-crossing gaps met all mitigation criteria.`

Calibrated interpretation: `Threshold crossing alone therefore provided an incomplete basis for intervention in the evaluated simulations.`

Unsafe interpretation: `Traditional fairness thresholds are fundamentally unreliable.`

## Audit procedure

Compare the source and revision on each applicable dimension:

1. What is the main proposition?
2. Who or what is being compared?
3. What is the direction and magnitude?
4. What uncertainty is expressed?
5. What study design and setting are implied?
6. What causal or mechanistic status is claimed?
7. What boundaries or exceptions are present?
8. Has any new fact, interpretation, or recommendation appeared?

If context is insufficient, preserve the ambiguity or ask for clarification. Do not silently resolve it.

## Audit result

Use one of three labels:

- `Pass`: all material dimensions are preserved;
- `Needs author review`: the revision is plausible but the source is ambiguous;
- `Fail`: at least one critical dimension changed.

A failed revision must not be returned as the recommended version.
