# Naturalness and Density Audit

The goal is not to imitate a detector or manufacture "human" imperfections. The goal is context-specific, professional, high-density research prose.

## Core standard

A strong revision should:

- sound like a researcher making a specific argument;
- retain domain terminology and substantive distinctions;
- remove filler, repetition, and empty signposting;
- vary syntax only where variation improves clarity;
- preserve necessary uncertainty and scope;
- avoid polished but low-information prose.

## 1. Generic framing audit

Review expressions such as:

- `In recent years`;
- `In today's rapidly evolving landscape`;
- `It is important to note that`;
- `It is worth noting that`;
- `It should be emphasized that`;
- `There is a growing need to`;
- `This highlights the importance of`.

These are not banned. Retain them only when they perform a necessary function.

Preferred repair order:

1. delete the frame;
2. replace it with a concrete development;
3. identify the affected setting, population, or decision;
4. state why the issue matters analytically.

## 2. Mechanical transition audit

Review repeated or inaccurate uses of:

`however`, `moreover`, `furthermore`, `additionally`, `therefore`, `consequently`, `nevertheless`, `overall`, and `taken together`.

For each marker, ask:

- Does the surrounding logic match the marker?
- Is the relation already clear without it?
- Has the same marker been used nearby?
- Would syntax express the relation more naturally?
- Is a full bridge sentence actually required?

Do not replace one repeated marker with a random synonym. Repair the relation.

## 3. Repetitive frame audit

Review repeated structures such as:

- `This study aims to...`;
- `This study seeks to...`;
- `Previous studies have shown...`;
- `The results show that...`;
- `These findings suggest that...`;
- `Future research should...`;
- `This study contributes to...`.

Possible repairs:

- change the grammatical subject;
- combine related findings;
- foreground the analytical object;
- use a marker-free transition;
- state the contribution directly;
- remove the sentence if it adds no information.

## 4. Over-explanation audit

Flag:

- a second sentence that merely paraphrases the first;
- definitions of familiar concepts that the audience does not need;
- summaries immediately after an already clear summary;
- multiple sentences explaining why a basic distinction matters;
- repeated reasons for the same methodological choice;
- meta-commentary about what the paragraph is doing;
- long courtesy formulas in reviewer responses.

Compression rule:

Keep the shortest version that preserves the argument, evidence, and necessary qualification.

## 5. High-density prose audit

High density means each sentence performs a clear analytical function. It does not mean long sentences.

Prefer:

- specific nouns and verbs;
- explicit analytical objects;
- one primary function per sentence;
- compact coordination of genuinely related findings;
- direct links between evidence and consequence.

Avoid:

- abstract noun chains;
- decorative complexity;
- stacked hedges;
- redundant topic sentences;
- empty closing sentences;
- repeating the same implication at multiple levels.

## 6. Specificity audit

Review vague nouns such as:

`issue`, `challenge`, `implication`, `insight`, `landscape`, `framework`, `approach`, `factor`, `aspect`, and `dimension`.

Ask:

- Which issue?
- Which decision?
- Which actor?
- Which population?
- Which mechanism?
- Which empirical setting?
- Which consequence?

Replace vague language only when the source text or context supports the more specific wording. Do not invent detail.

## 7. Sentence-opening and cadence audit

Check for:

- three or more consecutive sentences with the same subject pattern;
- uniform sentence length that creates mechanical rhythm;
- every paragraph following the same context-gap-solution shape;
- repeated three-item lists;
- every paragraph ending with a broad implication;
- excessive use of sentence adverbs;
- multiple highly symmetrical sentences with little new information.

Variation must remain functional. Do not rewrite a clear sentence merely to vary its opening.

## 8. Professional directness

Prefer direct but proportionate statements.

Over-softened:

`We sincerely thank the reviewer for this highly valuable and insightful comment.`

Direct:

`Thank you for raising this point.`

Overstated criticism:

`The method is fundamentally flawed.`

Proportionate criticism:

`The design does not separate temporal change from cohort composition.`

## 9. AI-detection notice

This audit reduces formulaic AI-style features. It does not guarantee any result from an AI detector.

Do not use or endorse claims such as:

- `AI detector safe`;
- `undetectable`;
- `detector-proof`;
- `guaranteed human score`;
- `below 15% AI`;
- `bypass detection`.

Acceptable statement:

`The revision reduces formulaic AI-style prose, but detector results vary across tools and may be incorrect. Final human review is required.`

## Final checklist

- [ ] No empty framing remains.
- [ ] Transition markers match the actual logic.
- [ ] Sentence openings are not mechanically repeated.
- [ ] Each sentence adds analytical information.
- [ ] No explanation is repeated without purpose.
- [ ] Vague abstractions have clear referents.
- [ ] Concision has not removed uncertainty or scope.
- [ ] The prose remains natural, professional, and discipline-appropriate.
- [ ] No detector-evasion promise is made.
