# Rhetorical Taxonomy

Use this taxonomy to identify what work a sentence or paragraph must perform before revising its language.

## 1. Context and problem framing

- `establish_relevance`: explain why the topic matters in a specific scholarly or practical context;
- `introduce_context`: provide the minimum background needed for the argument;
- `define_problem`: state the concrete problem to be addressed;
- `narrow_scope`: move from a broad domain to the study's analytical focus;
- `identify_practical_concern`: specify a decision, deployment, policy, or implementation problem;
- `identify_theoretical_concern`: specify an unresolved conceptual or explanatory issue.

## 2. Prior-work integration

- `summarize_research_stream`: describe the focus of a body of work;
- `synthesize_evidence`: combine findings into a shared pattern rather than list studies;
- `compare_approaches`: identify meaningful methodological or conceptual differences;
- `identify_consensus`: state where evidence converges;
- `identify_disagreement`: state where findings or interpretations diverge;
- `distinguish_concepts`: separate related but analytically distinct constructs.

## 3. Gap specification

- `attention_gap`: one issue has received less attention than another;
- `evidence_gap`: available evidence is limited, weak, mixed, or inconclusive;
- `methodological_gap`: prevailing designs cannot answer the target question well;
- `theoretical_gap`: existing theory does not explain or integrate a phenomenon;
- `mechanism_gap`: a pattern is documented but its generating process is unclear;
- `measurement_gap`: existing operationalizations do not capture the target construct adequately;
- `population_gap`: evidence is missing for a relevant group;
- `context_gap`: findings may not transfer across settings;
- `temporal_gap`: dynamic or longitudinal behavior remains unclear;
- `decision_gap`: measurement exists but action guidance is underdeveloped;
- `implementation_gap`: a method exists but operational use is unresolved;
- `validation_gap`: a framework lacks independent, external, or transportability testing.

Do not turn every limitation into a gap. A defensible gap must be specific, consequential, and supported by the surrounding literature.

## 4. Study positioning

- `state_aim`: define what the study examines or develops;
- `introduce_research_question`: state the question that organizes the analysis;
- `state_hypothesis`: present a testable expectation;
- `preview_design`: identify the design used to answer the question;
- `describe_scope`: clarify the population, context, or boundary of the study;
- `frame_contribution`: state what the work adds without inflated novelty;
- `distinguish_from_prior_work`: explain how the study changes the object, method, level, or decision problem.

## 5. Methodological reasoning

- `justify_design`: explain why a design fits the question;
- `define_construct`: clarify the conceptual meaning of a variable or index;
- `operationalize_measure`: connect a construct to its empirical measure;
- `describe_sample`: state the population, inclusion logic, and analytical sample;
- `sequence_analysis`: organize analyses by their inferential purpose;
- `motivate_model`: link model choice to the estimand or decision task;
- `introduce_baseline`: establish a meaningful comparison rule;
- `introduce_robustness_check`: test dependence on assumptions or specifications;
- `describe_sensitivity_analysis`: vary thresholds, samples, measures, or parameters;
- `explain_comparison`: state what a comparison can reveal.

## 6. Result navigation

- `introduce_main_result`: foreground the result that answers the main question;
- `report_pattern`: state the direction and form of a result;
- `compare_results`: report differences across models, groups, or conditions;
- `report_null_result`: describe weak or absent evidence without equating it with no effect;
- `report_exception`: identify a result that departs from the main pattern;
- `report_heterogeneity`: describe subgroup or contextual variation;
- `report_robustness`: state whether a result persists across checks;
- `refer_to_table_or_figure`: direct the reader to evidence without narrating every cell;
- `summarize_result_set`: combine related findings without collapsing important distinctions.

Keep interpretation out of the Results section when the target genre expects a separate Discussion.

## 7. Interpretation and explanation

- `interpret_result`: explain what a result means for the research question;
- `connect_to_theory`: relate the pattern to a theoretical expectation;
- `connect_to_prior_work`: compare the finding with existing evidence;
- `propose_mechanism`: offer an evidence-aligned process explanation;
- `consider_alternative`: identify another plausible account;
- `explain_discrepancy`: account for differences across studies or analyses;
- `state_implication`: identify the decision, theoretical, or methodological consequence.

## 8. Critical evaluation

- `identify_limitation`: specify a constraint and its consequence;
- `qualify_method`: state what an approach captures and what it misses;
- `challenge_assumption`: explain why an assumption may not hold;
- `identify_inference_limit`: define what cannot be concluded;
- `identify_design_constraint`: connect a design feature to restricted inference;
- `identify_missing_control`: identify a comparison or adjustment needed for interpretation.

Criticism should be specific, proportional, and directed at the evidence or reasoning rather than the authors.

## 9. Boundary and scope

- `define_applicability`: state where a claim or method is useful;
- `qualify_generalization`: limit transfer beyond the observed setting;
- `specify_boundary_condition`: identify conditions under which the pattern changes;
- `distinguish_supported_claim`: separate what the evidence supports from a stronger claim;
- `identify_transportability_limit`: explain why results may not transfer across populations or regimes.

## 10. Conclusion and future direction

- `synthesize_contribution`: state the integrated contribution rather than repeat the abstract;
- `state_theoretical_implication`: identify what changes in the conceptual account;
- `state_practical_implication`: identify who should do what differently and why;
- `identify_future_work`: connect future research to a specific unresolved issue;
- `close_argument`: end with the central analytical conclusion, not generic importance.

# Logical relations

Use one primary relation for each transition.

- `continuation`: extend the same line of reasoning;
- `addition`: introduce distinct but compatible information;
- `contrast`: show a difference;
- `concession`: acknowledge a valid point before limiting it;
- `qualification`: restrict scope or strength;
- `correction`: replace an inaccurate framing;
- `distinction`: separate related concepts;
- `comparison`: evaluate entities on a shared dimension;
- `cause`: state a supported causal relation;
- `consequence`: identify what follows from a prior condition;
- `specification`: make a broad claim more precise;
- `exemplification`: provide a representative case;
- `reformulation`: restate an idea more precisely;
- `synthesis`: combine multiple findings into an integrated conclusion;
- `interpretation`: move from observation to meaning;
- `analytical_sequence`: move between stages of analysis;
- `gap_to_objective`: connect an unresolved problem to the study response;
- `method_to_result`: connect an analysis to what it estimates;
- `result_to_interpretation`: move from evidence to a calibrated account;
- `interpretation_to_implication`: move from meaning to consequence;
- `limitation_to_future_work`: connect a constraint to a targeted next step.

# Transition selection rule

Prefer, in order:

1. lexical continuity when the relation is already clear;
2. parallel or contrastive syntax;
3. a subordinate clause;
4. one accurate transition marker;
5. a separate bridge sentence only when a real conceptual shift requires it.
