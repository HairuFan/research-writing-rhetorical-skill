# v1.0.0 Evaluation Report

## Scope

Version 1.0.0 was evaluated on 80 paired baseline-versus-skill cases formed by crossing 10 rhetorical tasks with 8 study-design conditions.

The compared conditions were:

1. the same base model without the skill;
2. the same base model with the skill enabled.

Generation used Claude Sonnet 5. Preliminary blind evaluation used Gemini 3.6 Flash in two independent runs with randomized A/B presentation. All inter-run disagreements were adjudicated by a human reviewer. Every case in which both judge runs preferred the baseline was also reviewed by the human adjudicator.

## Final adjudicated outcomes

| Outcome | Cases | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Skill preferred | 43 | 53.75% |
| Baseline preferred | 21 | 26.25% |
| Tie | 16 | 20.00% |
| Total | 80 | 100.00% |

Additional summary measures:

- skill win share among non-tied cases: **67.19%**;
- skill non-loss rate: **73.75%**;
- observed skill critical failures: **0**.

## Interpretation

The skill demonstrated a clear overall advantage over the baseline: it was preferred in roughly twice as many cases as the baseline and produced no observed critical failure involving meaning preservation, causal calibration, fabricated evidence, or unsupported methods.

The strongest and most stable gains appeared in:

- claim and causal calibration;
- evidential-boundary language for simulation and limitations;
- gap-to-objective transitions;
- reviewer-response directness;
- removal of generic framing and repetitive templates.

## Known limitations

The evaluation also identified several task-specific weaknesses:

1. **Boilerplate substitution.** Generic research-need language was sometimes replaced with equivalent formulas such as “motivates further investigation.”
2. **Unsupported narrowing.** Broad source language was occasionally narrowed to a particular domain, practice, design activity, or deployment context not supplied by the original.
3. **Contrast overinterpretation.** Literature-synthesis revisions sometimes inserted “whereas,” “while,” or “by contrast” even when the source established difference but not opposition.
4. **Parallel-structure degradation.** Some concise lists were made less effective through filler such as “a series of” or asymmetric phrasing.
5. **Minor emphasis loss.** Vague but material source emphasis, especially “important implications,” was occasionally weakened or removed.

These issues did not produce an observed critical failure in the evaluated set, but they are priorities for future releases.

## Release decision

Version 1.0.0 is approved as the first stable evaluated release.

The original comparative preference target of 70% was not reached: the final non-tied skill win share was 67.19%. This target is therefore retained as an improvement objective rather than represented as achieved. The release decision rests on the clear comparative benefit, zero observed critical failures, strong meaning preservation, and transparent documentation of remaining limitations.

## Reproducibility note

This is an internal paired benchmark rather than a claim of universal writing quality. Results may vary by generator, evaluator, prompt environment, and sampling. Future evaluations should include additional models, repeated generations, and independent human raters.
